# Khaevurzynth

> A sundial of white feathers measures the dream beneath an eyeless dragon's wing.

## The Garden That Kept Its Shadow

The garden lies behind a house with seven windows and no visible door. Its wall can be crossed by following the scent of wet leaves. Those who try to return by the same route find only ordinary brick.

At its center stands a sundial made of white feathers pressed into stone. Its shadow falls toward the sun. Each morning, the gardener brushes the stone with a branch of rosemary, gathering the previous day's light into a small clay dish.

He feeds that light to the roots of a tree whose leaves are black on both sides. The tree produces one fruit a year. It is shaped like a closed eyelid and remains warm until somebody asks whether it can be eaten.

The gardener never answers. He wraps the fruit in paper and places it inside the seventh window.

## A Visitor With a Paper Leaf

A visitor entered carrying a leaf folded from a page of her diary. She had found it on her pillow after dreaming of a garden in which every plant cast the same shadow.

The gardener unfolded it carefully. Its writing had vanished, but the creases formed a map of the garden. Where the sundial should have been, a little blank square remained.

She said she had written about an afternoon beneath a tree, a white bird landing close enough to touch, and a person who had asked her to look away before it flew. She could remember the request more clearly than the person's face.

The gardener set the paper against the sundial. The blank square filled with shadow. Above them, one of the house's windows opened, though no hand appeared at its latch.

## A Shadow With Seven Edges

The sundial's shadow divided into seven narrow shapes. Each reached a different window. Each carried a sound the visitor recognized without being able to place: a spoon set on wood, a curtain drawn, a chair moved closer to a bed.

She followed the quietest shape. At its window, the glass showed the afternoon from her missing diary page. The tree was younger. The white bird stood on the ground. Beside it waited someone holding a feather in a closed fist.

The visitor raised her hand. The figure did the same, but the feather remained in its other fist. When she moved closer, the glass became warm, and a faint line of scales appeared beneath her reflected sleeve.

Behind her, the gardener lifted his clay dish. Instead of light, it now held a little darkness shaped like a seed.

## What Slept Under the Sundial

The sundial stood above the folded wing of an eyeless dragon. Its feathers were not feathers at all, but the pale impressions left when the creature had tried to dream of birds.

Without eyes, it saw through shadows. Each window gave it a different hour. Each fruit from the black tree preserved an image it could not bear to let the sun erase.

The gardener had once tried to dig beneath the stone. His spade returned carrying a white feather and a little clay dish identical to his own. Since then, he had tended the garden with the careful uncertainty of someone walking beside a sleeper.

He placed the dark seed on the visitor's paper. Its roots followed the creases and entered the shadow-filled square. In the window, the figure's closed fist began to open.

## The Circle's Missing Hour

Along the sundial's edge, writing appeared between the pressed white shapes:

*Khae — vur — zynth — aluun.*

The visitor read it while keeping her eyes on the glass. The white bird lifted one wing. Beneath it lay a second feather, dark except for a narrow line of silver along its shaft.

The figure in the remembered afternoon offered its white feather to her. She reached for it and felt only paper. Her diary leaf had folded itself around a warmth that moved gently against her palm.

The seventh window opened. Within it rested the wrapped fruit from the previous year. Its paper loosened, revealing an eyelid carved from clear stone. Behind that closed lid, a small garden turned toward the first light of morning.

The sundial's seven shadows drew together. For one breath, their direction could not be decided.

## The Afternoon Left Undescribed

The visitor left through a gap she could not see until she stopped searching for it. Outside, the street smelled of rosemary after rain. Her folded page contained no feather, no seed, and no writing.

At home, she returned it to her diary. The next morning, an account of the afternoon had appeared in her own hand. It described a tree, a white bird, and a person whose name she had no difficulty reading. The last sentence remained blank.

The gardener continues to gather light from the sundial. The black tree still bears one warm fruit each year. Beneath the stone, the eyeless sleeper holds its wing over a patch of darkness where something small has begun to move.

And behind the seventh window, an unopened eye dreams of seeing without needing anyone to look away.
